# Day 21 - Capstone Project (Assignment 19)

**Date:** 28 Oct 2026
**Assignment:** 19 - Capstone Project

> All hands-on work was done only in the course's authorised capstone environment, for learning purposes.

## What the capstone was
The final assignment brings the whole course together in one end-to-end engagement, written up as a professional report.

## How I approached it
| Phase | What I applied | From module |
|---|---|---|
| Scoping | Read the brief and stayed strictly within the authorised targets | 6 (scope documents) |
| Reconnaissance | Passive and active information gathering, Nmap service discovery | 4, 6 |
| Vulnerability assessment | Matched services and versions to known weaknesses; tested the web application against the OWASP Top 10 with Burp | 5, 6 |
| Validation | Confirmed findings with minimal, read-only proof of concept | 5, 6 |
| Detection view | Thought about how each step would show up in logs and packet captures | 6, 10, 12 |
| Reporting | Wrote the report: summary, findings, impact, steps to reproduce, evidence, remediation | 5 (bug bounty reporting) |

## Report structure I used
1. Executive summary - the overall risk in plain language for non-technical readers.
2. Scope and methodology.
3. Findings table - title, severity, affected asset.
4. For each finding: description, impact, steps to reproduce, evidence (screenshots), recommended fix.
5. Remediation priorities and a retest plan.

## Key takeaways
- The capstone was the best test of the course: not one technique on its own, but the full lifecycle from the first scan to the remediation report.
- Writing the report took as much effort as the technical work, and it is the part a client actually reads.
